A binary tree works well for showing how doctors are connected in a hospital's reporting structure.
 It gives us a way to see how the hierarchy is organized, starting with the top doctor and moving down to other doctors.
 Each doctor can have a left and right child, which helps organize the relationships between them.

The three tree traversal methods are useful because they let us look at the structure in different ways.
 Preorder starts with the current doctor before moving to the left and right branches.
 This is helpful when we want to see the reporting structure from the top down. Inorder visits the left branch first,
 then the current doctor, and finally the right branch. This gives us another way to review the structure, although it
 does not automatically put the doctors in alphabetical order. Postorder visits the children before the current doctor, 
 which could be useful when we want to review the lower levels of the hierarchy before moving up to supervisors.

A min-heap makes sense for the emergency intake system because patients need to be prioritized based on how urgent their cases are. 
 Each patient receives an urgency score from 1 to 10, with 1 being the most urgent. The heap keeps the patient with the lowest number
 at the top, making it easier to identify who should be seen next. When a new patient is added or the current patient is removed, the 
 heap adjusts itself to keep the priorities organized. In a real hospital, staff would still need to follow proper medical triage procedures 
 to make sure patients receive the care they need.
